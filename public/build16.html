Building a 16mm camera from the salvaged parts

The lens is the part worth having. Everything below is about getting it pointed at a road and proving what it can actually read.

What 16mm actually buys

Horizontal field of view is 22.6° on a 1/2" sensor. At 1080p, against our two real thresholds — 120px of vehicle for the classifier to have a chance, and ~60px of plate before OCR can read characters:

DistanceScene widthVehiclePlate
15 m6.0 m576 px98 pxplate readable
25 m10.0 m346 px59 pxplate at the limit
40 m16.0 m216 px37 pxvehicle easily judged
60 m24.0 m144 px24 pxabove the bar, just
72 m28.8 m120 px20 pxthe bar exactly

For comparison: the reference camera medians 460px of vehicle, and every phone crop measured came in at 52–90px. This lens holds reference-camera quality out to roughly 25–30m, and stays useful to about 70m.

Narrower is not free. 22.6° covers only 12m of scene at 30m. This is a watch-one-stretch-of-road lens, not a watch-the-whole-intersection lens. Pick the stretch before you mount anything.

The one part to buy

A USB camera board with an M12 holder and NO IR-cut filter

Three requirements, and the third is the one people miss:

  • M12 / S-mount lens holder — so the 16mm screws straight on.
  • USB UVC output — camctl is already a console for a USB camera. A MIPI/Pi build means re-solving problems that are solved here.
  • No IR-cut filter (a "NoIR" board, or day/night with a removable filter) — an ordinary camera blocks 850nm and the IR ring will do nothing at all.

Arducam and ELP both sell 1080p USB M12 boards in NoIR versions. A smaller sensor crops the same lens narrower, which buys range at the cost of low-light performance: 1/2" → ~72m, 1/2.7" → ~86m, 1/3" → ~96m at the 120px bar.

Putting it together

1. Lens onto the board

Screw the 16mm into the M12 holder. Leave it loose — focus is set in step 4 and the lens has to turn.

2. IR ring in front

The ring has a centre hole for the lens to pass through; that is what it was built for. Feed the lens through and stand the ring off the board so it does not touch the barrel.

Check the voltage before powering it. The carrier board is silkscreened 12V LED, so 12V is likely, but read the ring itself. Wrong polarity kills IR LEDs instantly.

The round component next to the LEDs is probably a light sensor for automatic day/night. If it is, the ring switches itself on at dusk and needs no wiring beyond power.

3. Plug it in and confirm the machine sees it

python tools\probe_camera.py

Use CAP_MSMF, not CAP_DSHOW. DirectShow silently gives a lower resolution on this machine and the difference does not show up until crops arrive too small to judge.

4. Focus at the working distance, then lock it

Depth of field at 16mm is far less forgiving than a webcam. Focus on a car at the distance you actually care about, not on the far end of the street.

python tools\focus_sweep.py

Then tighten the grub screw on the M12 holder. Never leave autofocus on behind glass — it hunts between the pane and the street and every re-hunt is a second of blur that lands on the vehicle you wanted.

5. Aim it

At 22.6° you can miss a road entirely. Aim live:

python camctl\camctl.py     →  http://example-host:8160/

6. Prove it before trusting it

This is the step that decides whether the node is worth installing, and the numbers above are geometry — they assume perfect focus and no motion blur. Measure the real thing:

python tools\aim.py              # how far can THIS camera read a plate
python tools\resolution_floor.py # where recognition actually breaks
python tools\zoom_check.py       # in daylight: is any zoom real or fake

If aim.py disagrees with the table, believe aim.py.

7. Expose it for the light it has

python tools\auto_exposure.py

A fixed exposure is a daylight exposure. Measured at 23:00 on the reference camera: 83% of the frame near-black, and the detector posted 1–4 vehicles an hour against 200–300 in the afternoon. The map did not go quiet because traffic stopped.

About the night half

Six LEDs will not light 70m. Expect useful IR out to roughly 15–30m, so night range is a fraction of day range rather than the same number. Mount for the daytime stretch and accept a shorter night one, or add a second illuminator.

IR does not weaken the privacy guarantee. SEND_EDGE still caps every uploaded crop at 200px, so a private plate is exactly as illegible lit as unlit. What IR buys is seeing a vehicle at all after dark, plus readable government plates at close range, which is the tier that is public on purpose.

If the site has no WiFi

Only then is the Skywire worth the trouble. It is an AT-command modem in an XBee footprint, so it needs a carrier board, an activated SIM and a data plan, and CAT 1 modules are often locked to one carrier. Check the lock before buying a SIM.
