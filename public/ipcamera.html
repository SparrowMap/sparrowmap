Add an IP camera

If you already have a camera pointed at the street, it can put marked government vehicles on a public map without you buying anything or changing how the camera works.

Your camera stays yoursThe video never leaves your network. RavenMap never connects to your camera and is never given the password.
One small picture leavesA crop of a vehicle, shrunk below the size a number plate can be read at. The server refuses anything bigger.
You are not publishing a feedNo live view, no recordings, no footage of your customers or your door. A dot on a road, and a photo only after a person confirms it is a government vehicle.

What you need

A computer on the same network as the camera that can stay on: any Windows, Mac or Linux machine, including the one already running your recorder. It is not heavy work — measured at 0.02 to 0.04 seconds a frame on an ordinary processor, with no graphics card needed.

1 Where is the camera?

Drag the marker to the camera itself, not to the road. Which stretch of road it watches is set afterwards, on the aiming page.

3 Your camera's stream address

Every brand words it differently. If you do not know it, the first command below prints the usual shapes and tests whichever you try — it sends nothing anywhere.

Use the SUB stream where the camera offers one. A 4K main stream is wasted here and much harder to decode; the vehicle only needs to be a few hundred pixels across.
